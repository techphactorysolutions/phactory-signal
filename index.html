PHACTORY SIGNAL

From observation to response.

  1. Open a channel. Use the rear camera, import a video or photo, or explore the synthetic test signal. Allow camera access in Safari when asked.
  2. Choose your light. Tap the viewfinder to isolate a region. Adjust the threshold until the intended light is detected. Audio uses the video soundtrack; live microphone capture is optional.
  3. Read the pattern. Review measured RGB, pulse durations, prominent audio notes, and reference matches. A still photo has no timing or audio information.
  4. Compose a reply. Tap the colored keys, edit each step, and broadcast with the display and speakers. Both phones must share a pattern dictionary to assign meaning.
Add to your iPhone Home Screen

Understanding the measurements

Tracking uses brightness segmentation and connected regions, not semantic object identification. RGB values depend on exposure, white balance, clipping, compression, and downsampling; they do not measure an optical wavelength or spectrum.

Timing is limited by analyzed video frames, browser scheduling, and display refresh. The log reports frame timing and missed frames. FFT identifies a prominent frequency, which may be a harmonic; it is not reliable polyphonic transcription.

Notes assigned to colors are an editable creative convention. Reference matches cannot establish the source, intent, or meaning of an unknown signal. The five-tone preset is a cinematic reference; SOS uses International Morse.

No accounts, analytics, media uploads, or external runtime dependencies. Sessions and dictionaries are stored in this browser. Clearing website data removes them; export backups.
